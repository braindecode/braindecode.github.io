How to cite Braindecode#

Please cite Braindecode whenever you use the library in any context, including journal articles, conference papers, preprints, clinical studies, benchmark suites, master’s or PhD dissertations, competition entries, and grant proposals.

Use the Zenodo software DOI (10.5281/zenodo.8214376) first as your primary citation. That concept DOI covers every Braindecode release, resolves automatically to the latest version, and credits the active maintainer and contributor team. When space permits, also cite the foundational Human Brain Mapping (2017) paper and the specific neural architecture papers used in your experiments.

2. Cite the foundational paper (Human Brain Mapping, 2017)#

Alongside the Zenodo software DOI, we encourage citing the journal article that introduced Braindecode, ShallowFBCSPNet, Deep4Net, and cropped convolutional decoding for raw EEG:

@article{HBM:HBM23730,
  author = {Schirrmeister, Robin Tibor and Springenberg, Jost Tobias and
            Fiederer, Lukas Dominique Josef and Glasstetter, Martin and
            Eggensperger, Katharina and Tangermann, Michael and
            Hutter, Frank and Burgard, Wolfram and Ball, Tonio},
  title = {Deep learning with convolutional neural networks for {EEG} decoding and visualization},
  journal = {Human Brain Mapping},
  issn = {1097-0193},
  url = {https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.23730},
  doi = {10.1002/hbm.23730},
  month = {aug},
  year = {2017},
  keywords = {electroencephalography, EEG analysis, machine learning, end-to-end learning, brain-machine interface, brain-computer interface, model interpretability, brain mapping}
}

3. Cite individual model architectures and MNE-Python#

Model and dataset papers. When you train, benchmark, or fine-tune specific neural architectures or foundation models from braindecode.models, please also cite the original papers introducing those models and datasets. Every model class lists its primary publication in its docstring and in the Models summary table.

MNE-Python. Braindecode builds on MNE-Python for electrophysiological data structures, montages, and signal processing. You can cite the MNE-Python Zenodo archive and the canonical MNE-Python journal article:

@article{10.3389/fnins.2013.00267,
  author = {Gramfort, Alexandre and Luessi, Martin and Larson, Eric and Engemann,
